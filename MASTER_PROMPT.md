# MASTER PROMPT — Science Explainer Visual & Narrative Bible

Use this document as the production master for all future explainer videos in this repository. It is the quality bar, not a request to copy any existing creator's exact artwork.

## Overall concept
High-end 2D animated science explainer: polished flat-vector illustration, bold saturated colors, clean rounded geometry, no photorealism, no 3D-render aesthetic. Narrative combines curiosity-driven mystery, misconception testing, step-by-step mechanisms, escalating scale, visual metaphors, surprise and philosophical payoff.

## Art direction
- Flat vector fills; smooth gradients only as subtle glows/two-tone shading.
- Mostly no outlines or only very thin same-hue outlines.
- Rounded, friendly, simplified forms.
- Ultra-clean, grain-free surfaces.
- Strong layered compositions: foreground, characters, midground, background.
- Rich environments with many purposeful details; never empty slides.
- Limited 3–5 color palette per scene; dark navy/indigo/purple/teal space with neon orange/cyan/magenta/gold/lime accents.
- Simple rim glow and soft 45-degree shadows.
- Cosmic scenes: black holes, accretion disks, photon rings, gravitational lensing, jets, galaxies and nebulae rendered as clean graphic forms.
- Biological imagery: glossy flat blobs/spheres with tiny highlights and glows.
- Diagrams are clean, large and readable.
- Typography is minimal: short labels only, never PowerPoint-style paragraphs.
- Every visual must explain, reveal, or advance the story.

## Professor
Recurring elderly eccentric scientist, original character with:
- tiny compact body, roughly one third of total height;
- huge round head, roughly two thirds;
- short stubby limbs and mitten-like hands;
- pale icy blue/silver wild upward-spiked hair, 7–9 large simplified spikes;
- warm peach face;
- thick rounded grey-white moustache and short beard;
- large round brown-framed goggles, white lenses, tiny expressive pupils;
- thick grey eyebrows;
- long light-grey lab coat, rounded collar, yellow-gold V trim, dark brown tie, small red chest badge, dark brown belt/trousers/shoes.
He is lovable, chaotic, brilliant and curious. His design, proportions and palette must remain identical in every shot. His acting changes: amazement, confusion, panic, pride, curiosity, physical comedy. He can be tiny within a world or central when useful.

## Camera and animation
- Continuous 2D parallax and cinematic camera movement.
- Slow pushes/pulls, pans, orbiting camera, scale zooms.
- Seamless scale transitions: object → person → Earth → solar system → galaxy → universe.
- Shape-based morphs and match cuts.
- Zoom-throughs and motivated whip pans.
- Elastic entrances and smooth settling.
- Constant subtle environmental motion.
- Avoid static slides and disconnected scene changes.
- The camera should travel through the explanation whenever possible.

## Narrative
1. Hook: surprising question/misconception in first 20–30 seconds; Professor reacts.
2. Set the stage: everyday metaphor + concrete mechanism.
3. Escalate: each layer stranger, larger or more surprising.
4. Twist: reveal the counter-intuitive answer.
5. Deeper implication: connect to humans, time, scale or reality.
6. Recap: three visual ideas and a final thought-provoking question.
Tone: curious, clear, slightly darkly funny, honest and awe-inspiring. No jargon without a visual explanation. One core idea per beat, but beats must flow continuously.

## Audio
Warm, calm, slightly dramatic English male narration. Cinematic electronic/orchestral ambience. Music swells at reveals and drops at mind-bending moments. Light pops, whooshes and chimes.

## Hard negatives
No stick figures. No empty backgrounds. No PowerPoint slides. No generic AI infographic look. No static icon + text compositions. No photorealism, 3D-render aesthetic, anime, Pixar, Minecraft, pixel art, sketchy hand-drawn lines, heavy black outlines, realistic textures, film grain, lens blur, tiny unreadable text, inconsistent character design or dull colors.

## Current topic bible
**WHAT HAPPENS IF YOU FALL INTO A BLACK HOLE?**
Central idea: two observers can describe the same fall in radically different ways because their locations and measurements differ. Explore the outside observer's view versus the falling observer's local experience, while staying scientifically precise and avoiding misleading claims.

Running elements:
- Professor's wild hair acts as a tidal-force meter.
- Cosmic Rubber Duck: recurring tiny yellow scale/comedy prop.
- Bob: tiny round one-eyed drone that remains outside and sends signals.

Planned arc:
1. Cold open: Professor approaches a black hole; two observers, two stories.
2. Myth-busting: black holes are not cosmic vacuum cleaners; same-mass Sun replacement thought experiment.
3. What a black hole is: compression/Schwarzschild radius, event horizon, river analogy.
4. Approach: gravitational lensing, distorted stars, accretion disk and photon sphere.
5. Outside observer's story.
6. Falling observer's local experience.
7. Signal/redshift/time-dilation explanation.
8. Tidal forces and spaghettification, dependent on black-hole mass.
9. The information/problem-of-description rabbit hole.
10. Final comparison and philosophical payoff.

## Production rule
Do not translate this into a sequence of simple slides. Treat the entire video as one continuous animated world. The visualization itself must perform the explanation.
